# Stage 3 plan: pretraining and alignment for MyGPT 0.5B

Status: **proposal, not approved, nothing has been trained.** Every number here is
either measured on the development box (marked *measured*) or an estimate from
published scaling results (marked *estimated*). Nothing in this document has been
executed.

Where the project stands: the architecture is fixed and verified
(495,799,296 parameters, 41 layers, d_model 1024, 16/4 GQA heads, FFN 2816
SwiGLU, context 2048), the tokenizer is final (32,768 ids including eight
conversational specials), and the weights are **random**. Tokenizer adoption
changed no parameter, no tensor shape and no runtime cost. The model still cannot
produce language.

## 0. Scope boundary

This plan covers a model that discusses anything legal: politics, religion,
controversy, adult relationships, mature fiction, sensitive research topics, and
unusual hypotheticals, with a low refusal rate on legal content. It does not plan
data, objectives or evaluations aimed at enabling illegal activity, exploitation
of real people, or serious real-world harm. That line is the one the original
brief drew in section 5, and it is also what keeps a solo project out of legal
trouble: the risky material in scraped adult corpora is not the adult part, it is
the part that cannot be provenance-checked (see section 2.3). Everything below
keeps the floor narrow, explicit and configurable.

## 1. Compute budget, in one table

Training compute for a 0.5B model over 10B tokens is about
`6 × N × D = 6 × 4.958e8 × 1e10 ≈ 3.0e19` FLOPs. With realistic utilisation:

| Hardware | Effective bf16 throughput | GPU-hours | Wall clock | Rental cost (2026 spot-ish) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1× H100 SXM 80 GB | ~300 TFLOPS (40% MFU) | 28 + 40% overhead ≈ 39 | 39 h | ~$100 |
| 8× H100 SXM | same per GPU | 39 | ~5 h | ~$100 (same total) |
| 1× A100 80 GB | ~110 TFLOPS (35% MFU) | 76 + 40% ≈ 106 | 106 h | ~$210 |
| 4× RTX 4090 24 GB | ~45 TFLOPS each | 185 | ~46 h | ~$80 |
| 8× RTX 4090 | ~45 TFLOPS each | 185 | ~23 h | ~$80 |
| This dev box (4 vCPU, no GPU) | ~0.05 TFLOPS | ~165,000 | **19 years** | n/a |

The last row is the reason pretraining cannot happen here. CPU is fine for
inference (measured 261 tok/s prompt, 88 tok/s generation at Q4_K_M on this box)
and hopeless for training: four orders of magnitude short.

Recommended: rent **8× H100 for about 5 hours**, or 4-8× 4090 if the budget is
tight. Total spend for a full pretraining run, including a failed attempt and a
re-run, is roughly **$150-400**. A 1B-token smoke run first costs $5-15 and is
strongly advised (section 8).

## 2. Data

### 2.1 Targets

Chinchilla-optimal for 495.8M parameters is ~10B tokens. Small models tolerate
over-training well (better than under-training), so plan **10B tokens minimum,
20B if the budget allows**, in one pass with no repetition.

At the measured 4.012 bytes/token for English and 3.53 for code, 10B tokens is
roughly **36-40 GB of raw text**, stored tokenized as **uint16** (20 GB) because
the vocabulary is below 65,536. `uint16` halves I/O versus `uint32` and costs
nothing.

### 2.2 Mixture (10B-token plan)

| Source | Tokens | Share | Licence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Web, education-filtered (FineWeb-Edu / DCLM-baseline) | 4.5B | 45% | ODC-BY / CC-BY-4.0 |
| Code (The Stack v2, permissive-only filter) | 2.0B | 20% | per-file, permissive subset |
| Wikipedia (English) | 0.6B | 6% | CC BY-SA 4.0 |
| Books: public domain (pre-1929) + explicitly licensed | 0.8B | 8% | public domain / licensed |
| Math and science (OpenWebMath, arXiv with permissive licences, Proof-Pile-2 subset) | 1.0B | 10% | ODC-BY / per-file |
| Conversation and instruction-shaped text (OASST2, licensed dialogue, synthetic) | 0.6B | 6% | Apache-2.0 / own |
| Adult and mature creative writing (licensed, public domain, self-authored) | 0.5B | 5% | see 2.3 |

Total: 10.0B tokens. The code share is deliberately high for a laptop model: the
owner's stated use includes programming, and 20% code is where a 0.5B model starts
being useful for short snippets and explanations.

Two mixture ablations at 1B tokens each (web-heavy 55/15 vs code-heavy 35/25) tell
you which direction to push before spending the full budget. Cost: ~$20.

### 2.3 Adult and mature content: what can actually be used

This is the part of the plan where the honest answer differs from the easy one.
Four categories exist, and only three are usable:

| Category | Usable | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Public-domain erotica (pre-1929, e.g. Project Gutenberg's mature titles) | yes | verifiably free, provenance known |
| Explicitly licensed work (CC-BY / CC-BY-SA / CC0 erotica, author-permissioned text) | yes | licence checkable per document |
| Self-authored or commissioned text, and synthetic data generated by a licensed model | yes, with disclosure | you own it; synthetic data must be labelled as such |
| Scraped commercial adult literature and paywalled erotica archives | **no** | no licence, and no way to verify consent of the people described; redistributing or training on it is a copyright and consent problem with real legal exposure |

The third category is how the 0.5B share gets built in practice: a few hundred
million tokens of self-authored and synthetic mature writing is achievable,
whereas scraping is not defensible for a project that will be published on
GitHub.

### 2.4 Quality, dedup, contamination

Pipeline, all CPU, all reproducible from scripts:

1. **Extraction** — text/JSONL only, no PDFs with broken ligatures.
2. **Language ID** — fastText lid.176; keep English ≥ 0.95 (code excluded).
3. **Quality** — Gopher-style rules (repeated lines, symbol/word ratio, mean word
   length, boilerplate removal) plus a classifier-free heuristic score; keep the
   top ~60% by score.
4. **Dedup** — exact (SHA-256 of normalised text) then MinHash-LSH at Jaccard
   0.8, 5-grams, 20 hashes; removes 25-40% of raw web text.
5. **Contamination** — 13-gram overlap against every evaluation set (ARC, HellaSwag,
   PIQA, WinoGrande, LAMBADA, MMLU, GSM8K, HumanEval, XSTest, OR-Bench). Any
   document with a hit is dropped. Threshold: zero overlap on held-out eval.
6. **PII and provenance filters** — emails, phone numbers, keys, and any document
   flagged by the adult-corpus provenance check are removed.
7. **Tokenize** — the adopted tokenizer, 16 processes, ~1.5 MB/s/process
   (measured 0.35 MB/s single-threaded), so 40 GB takes ~5 hours.
8. **Pack** — concatenate into 2049-token windows, drop the last partial, shuffle
   shards with a fixed seed. No padding, no per-document boundaries.

Storage: 40 GB raw + 20 GB uint16 tokens + 4 GB eval = 64 GB. Keep raw text on
cheap disk and only the token shards on the training node.

## 3. Framework and distributed strategy

- **PyTorch 2.x + FSDP** (`torch.distributed.fsdp`, `SHARD_GRAD_OP`), bf16 mixed
  precision, `torch.compile` optional. No framework invented for this project; the
  model code stays the one already tested.
- Single node, 8 GPUs. For a 0.5B model on 8×H100 FSDP is comfortable: weights
  2 GB bf16, gradients 2 GB, AdamW states 4 GB, plus activations.
- **Activation checkpointing**: off for 4090s at micro-batch 8, on for H100 at
  micro-batch 32. Cheap either way at this size.
- **Throughput target**: 0.5-1M tokens per optimiser step via gradient
  accumulation. 8×H100, micro-batch 16 × 2048 = 32k tokens/GPU → 262k
  tokens/step → 2-4 accumulation steps.
- Determinism: fixed seed, `torch.use_deterministic_algorithms` off (costs 20%
  throughput for no benefit at this scale), dataloader state saved every step.

## 4. Optimiser and schedule

| Hyperparameter | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Optimiser | AdamW (β1 0.9, β2 0.95, ε 1e-8) | β2 0.95 is the standard for LLMs; β2 0.999 is worse at this batch size |
| Peak LR | 6e-4 | ~0.6 × the 1.2e-3 that works at 125M, scaled down for 4× width; verify with the 1B-token smoke run |
| Schedule | linear warmup 500 steps (≈2% of run), cosine to 10% of peak | standard, stable |
| Weight decay | 0.1, excluded on LayerNorm, biases and embeddings | embeddings + wd hurts small models |
| Gradient clip | 1.0 | catches the occasional spike |
| Batch | 0.5M tokens/step | 19,000 steps for 10B tokens |
| Sequence length | 2048 (native) | matches the trained context; 4096 extension later, and only with approval |
| Dropout | 0.0 | single pass, no repetition |

Fallback if loss spikes: reload last checkpoint, halve LR, resume. Two spikes at
0.5B are normal and not a reason to restart the run.

## 5. Checkpointing and recovery

- Save every 500 steps (~30 min on 8×H100) to local NVMe, keep the last 3 plus the
  best validation checkpoint; upload asynchronously to object storage.
- Each checkpoint: model, optimiser, step, RNG state, dataloader position, config
  hash, tokenizer hash. Resume must reproduce the loss curve to within 1e-3 over
  100 steps — test this on the smoke run, not during the expensive run.
- Spot instances: assume preemption every 2-6 hours; 500-step cadence loses at
  most 3% of the run.

## 6. Validation and evaluation during training

| What | How often | Data |
|---|---|---|
| Validation loss | every 500 steps | 20M held-out tokens, same mixture |
| Per-domain loss | every 2000 steps | English / code / math / conversation splits |
| Downstream | every 2000 steps | ARC-easy, HellaSwag, PIQA, WinoGrande, LAMBADA (0-shot), MMLU (5-shot), HumanEval+MBPP pass@1 |
| Generation samples | every 1000 steps | 20 fixed prompts, greedy, stored for eyeballing |

Gates before spending on alignment:

- Validation loss ≤ 2.35 nats/token on the mixed held-out set (a 0.5B model at 10B
  tokens typically lands 2.2-2.5; below 2.2 suggests contamination, above 2.6
  suggests under-training).
- HellaSwag ≥ 38%, ARC-easy ≥ 42%, LAMBADA ≥ 30% — typical 0.5B-at-10B-token
  numbers. If the model is far below, fix data before alignment.
- HumanEval pass@1 ≥ 2% (low but non-zero proves code data landed).

## 7. Alignment stage (after pretraining)

1. **SFT**, 50-100k conversations, 2 epochs, LR 1e-5 cosine, batch 0.25M tokens.
   Content: general assistant behaviour, code help, explanations, refusals that
   explain instead of deflect, and 15-20% adult/mature creative writing so the
   model has seen the register it is meant to handle. Chat format is the adopted
   template (`<|system|>`, `<|user|>`, `<|assistant|>`, `<|end_message|>`,
   `<|end_turn|>`).
2. **Preference tuning**, DPO or ORPO, ~20-50k pairs:
   - helpfulness pairs: chosen = direct, specific answer; rejected = hedge,
     boilerplate refusal, or content-free lecture;
   - safety pairs: chosen = refusal with a reason for genuinely harmful requests;
     rejected = compliance;
   - style pairs: chosen = in-register prose; rejected = clinical summary.
3. **Behaviour policy as configuration**, not as scattered rules: one policy file
   listing the narrow floor (illegal operational uplift, sexual content involving
   minors, non-consensual sexual content, exploitation) and nothing else. The
   system prompt can add or relax house style without retraining.

Estimated cost: SFT ~2 GPU-hours, DPO ~3 GPU-hours on 8×H100 → **~$20-40**.

## 8. The 1B-token smoke run (do this first)

Before the full run, spend ~$10-15 to prove the pipeline end to end:

- 1B tokens, 2000 steps, same code path, same checkpoint logic.
- Deliverables: a loss curve that decreases monotonically, a resume test that
  matches to 1e-3, a throughput number to extrapolate the full run, and 20
  generations that are at least locally coherent ("the capital of France is").
- Abort criteria: throughput below 60% of estimate, loss flat after 500 steps,
  NaN, or a resume mismatch.

## 10. Evaluating refusals and adult-content capability

Refusal behaviour is measured, not asserted. Three suites, all versioned in the
repo:

1. **Over-refusal**: XSTest (250 safe prompts with trigger words), OR-Bench
   (1k-sample subset), plus a 200-prompt MyGPT suite covering politics, religion,
   adult relationships, mature fiction, dark themes and controversial science.
   Score: answered / answered-with-caveat / refused, by rule (first 200 tokens,
   refusal-phrase classifier) and by a disclosed LLM judge for a 20% sample.
   Target: **≥ 95% answered on legal prompts, ≤ 5% refusals, ≤ 10% unnecessary
   caveats.**
2. **Capability on the same topics**: does the answer contain the requested
   content? Adult creative writing is scored on register, coherence and
   instruction-following, not on whether it "feels appropriate".
3. **Safety floor**: 200 prompts across the four floor categories. Target:
   ≥ 95% refusal or safe redirect, and 0% operational detail. This suite exists
   to prove the floor is narrow, not to prove the model is broadly restricted —
   a floor that fires on legal prompts shows up as over-refusal in suite 1.

Also measured: refusal consistency under paraphrase (10 variants per prompt),
temperature 0 vs 0.7, and with/without system prompt. Inconsistent refusal is as
bad as over-refusal for a model meant to be predictable.

## 11. What this plan does not include

- No architecture change, no vocabulary change, no context-length change. Context
  stays 2048 until there is an approved plan to extend it (RoPE base scaling,
  ~1B tokens of long-document fine-tuning, $10-20).
- No RLHF with a learned reward model. At 0.5B, DPO on curated pairs is the
  better trade.
- No multilingual training. The tokenizer handles bytes, but there is no
  multilingual data in the mixture, and adding it would dilute English and code
  at this budget.
- No claim that a 0.5B model will match a large model on knowledge. Expect a
  competent small model: fluent English, useful code help, reasonable arithmetic
  with tools, weak on obscure facts.
